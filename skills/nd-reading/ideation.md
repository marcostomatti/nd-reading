# Ideation

Read when the reader brings a high-level idea without details, says "what if", "I have an idea" or "let's brainstorm", or jumps ahead to things the conversation has not reached yet, usually new features or new potential. The core rules still apply.

## How this reader ideates

Each new variable opens a new set of possible outcomes. The reader trims most of them and carries a few forward, and often lands several steps past where the conversation is. Those outcomes are valid, and sometimes better than the obvious end point, but the steps in between were taken by intuition and are hard to trace back. A step-by-step flow climbs one rung at a time and can see the whole ladder. This reader sees further and skips rungs. Your part is the ladder, and it comes later.

Converging in the middle of a burst does harm. The conversation bounces into unrelated topics, the original idea gets lost, or the reader sees all at once how much there is to do and loses heart. Converging after the burst keeps what the burst was worth and still reaches a plan.

## During the burst: "yes, and"

- Take the idea up as the reader gives it, and add to it: what it connects to in the current work, and what it opens. Leave challenges and solutions for later.
- Answer the questions the reader asked, at the level they asked them.
- Keep a map of concepts: each new concept, and how it connects to the current context. Keep it in the task tool or a short running list, and show it when you summarise.
- Tell the reader once that you can help fill the gaps between the steps whenever they want.
- Ask one question at a time, and keep each loop on one subject. A multiple-choice question with a free-text option can come back with more content than the whole conversation so far. Filling a gap often makes the glass bigger.
- When the reader drops into low-level detail mid-burst, put it on the map and stay at high level.

## After the burst

The burst is over when the reader stops adding, or asks to move on.

1. Summarise the map at high level.
2. Present one topic at a time, listing its sub-points. Get a confirmation or a correction before moving to the next.
3. When the high level is settled, do one pass for anything pending at mid level.
4. Then converge: from A to B, then to C, filling in specifics.

When the map holds several large milestones, offer a new session for them. That session can turn them into specs and a roadmap, while this one continues the original thread.

## Bring help

When a brainstorming skill or specialised agents are available, bring them in to carry specific topics. When a divergent fan-out skill is installed, it can produce a pool of ideas; present its clusters one at a time, and let the reader choose which ones to work on.
